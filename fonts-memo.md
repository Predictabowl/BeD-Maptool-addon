## Alternatives to Morris Roman (Celtic / medieval uncial look)
Morris Roman is based on William Morris's 1893 Kelmscott Press lettering — rounded, ornate, semi-uncial capitals with a hand-lettered medieval feel. Closest Google Fonts matches:

- **Uncial Antiqua** — the closest match by far. Genuine uncial-script letterforms, same medieval-manuscript feel.
- **MedievalSharp** — hand-drawn medieval manuscript style, very popular for RPG/fantasy UI (you'll recognize it from a lot of D&D tools).
- **Metamorphous** — bold, sturdy, slightly Celtic/runic character shapes.
- **Almendra** / **Almendra Display** — old-style serif with a slightly gothic, illuminated-manuscript quality, good at body-text sizes too since Almendra (non-display) is more readable.
- **IM Fell English** / **IM Fell Great Primer** — based on actual 17th-century English printing types, very authentic "old book" feel, and readable in longer passages.
- **Cinzel** / **Cinzel Decorative** — inspired by classical Roman inscriptional capitals rather than medieval manuscripts, more "ancient stone" than "illuminated parchment," but pairs beautifully as a heading font next to something like Almendra for body text.

## Alternatives to "Vecna" (dark / sinister / lich-evil vibe)
Since Vecna isn't itself a named typeface — I'm reading this as "something with that ominous, ancient-evil D&D villain feel." A few directions depending on how literal vs. stylized you want it:

- **UnifrakturMaguntia** / **UnifrakturCook** — true blackletter/fraktur, reads as ancient and menacing without tipping into cartoonish horror.
- **Pirata One** — blackletter-adjacent but a bit more compact and legible at small sizes, good for UI chrome rather than just big headers.
- **Nosifer** — dripping, jagged, overtly "evil villain" styling — this is the one that reads as horror-poster rather than old-manuscript.
- **Eater** — similarly aggressive/decayed look, a bit more textured/grungy.
- **Griffy** — quirky, slightly gnarled serif, reads as "old and unsettling" rather than "dripping blood," more subtle than Nosifer/Eater.

If you want a suggestion on pairing: **Cinzel Decorative** or **Uncial Antiqua** for section headers/titles, paired with **Almendra** or **IM Fell English** for body text, gives you a cohesive "ancient tome" look without everything screaming for attention at once. If Vecna needs to feel like the odd one out (the corrupting evil in an otherwise clean design), **Nosifer** or **UnifrakturMaguntia** used sparingly for his name/title only would do that well.
